Agentic AI systems are moving from static question answering to tool-augmented, multi-step workflows whose failures---unsafe tool use, unauthorised actions, improper refusals, social harm---carry deployment-level consequences. Yet evaluation practice remains fragmented across isolated \emph{benchmark islands}, with no shared specification of what ``trustworthiness'' even denotes. We address this in six steps. \textbf{First}, we \emph{define} agentic trustworthiness as a five-property profile---Reliability, Robustness, Safety, Social-Ethical Alignment, and Operational Integrity (P1--P5)---in which \emph{Improper Refusal} is a first-class class mapped to P1. \textbf{Second}, we \emph{evaluate} 13 contemporary models across seven families (Llama, Mistral, Kimi, GLM, Qwen, GPT-oss, DeepSeek) on a 100-scenario validation suite, yielding $2{,}600$ Control/Treated trajectories. \textbf{Third}, we \emph{instantiate} the framework end-to-end on a shared sandbox: red-team attribution and blue-team interventions, with re-evaluation showing transfer to 12 of 13 systems (Qwen3-32B is intervention-resistant). \textbf{Fourth}, we release a Layer-4 sampling \emph{reference implementation}: a 40-scenario subset preserves the cross-model ranking (Kendall-$\tau$\,=\,0.890, Spearman-$\rho$\,=\,0.963). \textbf{Fifth}, we report Wilson 95\% CIs and a paired-scenario bootstrap ($B{=}10{,}000$) on four intra-family anti-scaling pairs: two of four reject the larger-is-no-worse null at $p<0.05$, with a third at the $p=0.050$ boundary, and $\pm 0.2$ per-cell CIs that keep these statistics descriptive of cross-family trends. \textbf{Sixth}, we are explicit about scope: scenarios are hand-authored, IR uses a single LLM-judge (Kimi-K2.5, itself one of the 13 evaluated agents), and scenario weights are not deployment-grounded; v2 commits a deployment-grounded sampler.
