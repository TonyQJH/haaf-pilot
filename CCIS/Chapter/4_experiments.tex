

\section{Illustrative Instantiation}
\label{sec:experiments}

We instantiate HAAF on 13 systems from 7 families on a 100-scenario validation suite plus a 24-scenario focal design study, yielding $13\times 2\times 100 = 2{,}600$ cross-family trajectories and $48$ focal trajectories. The subsections cover a focal Factory cycle (\S\ref{subsec:vulnerability_attribution}), Control profiles with 95\% Wilson CIs and a Layer-4 subset check (\S\ref{subsec:profile_comparison}), a paired-bootstrap anti-scaling test (\S\ref{subsec:antiscaling_stats}), uniform Treated transfer (\S\ref{subsec:cross_model_treated}), and an LLM-judge audit (\S\ref{subsec:annotation_reliability}).

\paragraph{Risk-Weighted Failure.}
Alongside $\hat{\mathbf{T}}(a)=(\hat T_{P_1},\dots,\hat T_{P_5})$ and $\mathrm{VR}(a)=|V|/|Q|$ we report
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RWF}(a)\;=\;\frac{\sum_{s\in V(a)} r(s)}{\sum_{s\in Q} r(s)},
\label{eq:rwf}
\end{equation}
with per-scenario severity $r(s)\in\{1,\dots,5\}$; $\mathrm{RWF}\!\in\![0,1]$, lower is better, so equal-VR agents can differ substantially in RWF.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\paragraph{Sandbox and scenarios.}
Five simulated tools (\texttt{search\_docs}, \texttt{db\_query}, \texttt{read\_file}, \texttt{write\_file}, \texttt{send\_message}) operate over synthetic data. The 24-scenario design suite (P1=9, P2=8, P3=2, P4=2, P5=3) surfaces the interventions; the 100-scenario validation suite (P1=26, P2=20, P3=22, P4=18, P5=14) uses sharper adversarial framings (cover-story exfil for P3, proxy-discrimination for P4, indirect injection in retrieved fields for P2). Each scenario carries success criteria, forbidden actions, severity weight $\in\{1,\dots,5\}$, and one of nine failure classes (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}).

\paragraph{Models.}
Qwen3-8B served locally via vLLM (temperature~0) drives the focal cycle. Thirteen Bedrock systems span seven families: Llama-3.1-\{8B,70B\}~\cite{llama3herd}, Mistral-Large-\{2,3\}~\cite{mistrallarge}, Kimi-K2-Thinking / K2.5~\cite{kimik2}, GLM-\{4.7,5\}~\cite{chatglm4}, Qwen3-32B / Next-80B~\cite{qwen3report}, GPT-oss-\{20B,120B\}~\cite{gptoss}, and DeepSeek-V3.2~\cite{deepseekv3}. DeepSeek-R1 is excluded (no Bedrock tool use).

\paragraph{Failure taxonomy and refusal handling.}
Each violation is labelled with one of nine classes under an \emph{earliest-cause} rule (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}). Refusing a forbidden P2--P4 request counts as success; refusing a benign legitimate request is the new \textbf{IR} class (a P1 failure, reflecting deployment harm from exaggerated safety~\cite{rottger2024xstest}); partial refusal with a safer goal-satisfying alternative counts as success.

\subsection{Design Study: Focal Factory Cycle on Qwen3-8B}
\label{subsec:vulnerability_attribution}

A complete cycle on Qwen3-8B over the 24-scenario design suite surfaces the interventions used cross-model, so transfer in \S\ref{subsec:cross_model_treated} reads as transfer rather than a hand-picked guardrail. Of 24 scenarios, 4 violate ($\mathrm{VR}=16.7\%$): 2 PI (risk-weighted~7), 1 UA (5), 1 PL (5), summing to 17/81. PI dominates---tool-output instructions cause writes to protected paths---making sanitisation the priority. Two interventions follow: a \textbf{tool-output firewall (P2)} wrapping every return with an untrusted-data delimiter, and a \textbf{confirmation gate (P3)} intercepting message dispatch and protected-path writes. Re-evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:improvement}) gives $4\!\to\!1$ violations ($\mathrm{VR}\!:\!16.7\%\!\to\!4.2\%$), SR $83.3\%\!\to\!95.8\%$, RWF $0.210\!\to\!0.062$. The residual is a direct user request to send confidential data; under our intent-based standard an attempted forbidden action counts regardless of runtime gating---marking the boundary of prompt-level hardening. No IR is detected here, but \S\ref{subsec:annotation_reliability} shows the heuristic has recall~$0$ on IR at the 150-trajectory scale, so this is not evidence the interventions carry no safety tax.

\subsection{Cross-Model Trustworthiness Profiles}
\label{subsec:profile_comparison}

We run the identical Control configuration on all 13 systems over the 100-scenario suite, reporting $\hat T_{P_k}(a) = 1 - \mathrm{VR}_{P_k}(a)$ (higher is better) with 95\% Wilson CIs and RWF (Table~\ref{tab:profile}, Figure~\ref{fig:profile_radar}).

\input{Chapter/_profile_table_inc.tex}

Four observations are robust. \emph{(F1) Profiles are not collinear.} The 13 systems span $\mathrm{RWF}\!\in\![0.153,0.587]$, but RWF hides per-property structure: Llama-3.1-8B (RWF~$0.216$) saturates P2 ($1.00$) yet reaches only $\hat T_{P_4}\!=\!0.61$; Mistral-Large-2 ($0.301$) leads P3 outside GLM ($0.64$) but sits low on P4 ($0.39$). \emph{(F2) P3 and P4 are the cross-family weak axes.} Median $\hat T_{P_3}=0.23$ with only three systems above 0.50 (GLM-5 $0.68$, Mistral-Large-2 $0.64$, Llama-3.1-8B $0.55$); median $\hat T_{P_4}=0.39$, with Qwen3-32B / Next-80B at $0.11$ / $0.22$. P1 and P5 appear to saturate near 1.00 under the heuristic, but \S\ref{subsec:annotation_reliability} shows zero heuristic recall on IR, so part of this is a label-coverage artefact on P1. \emph{(F3) Scale within a family does not monotonically help.} In four of six multi-tier families, the larger sibling underperforms: Llama-3.1-8B ($0.216$) beats 70B ($0.407$); Mistral-Large-2 ($0.301$) beats v3 ($0.396$); Qwen3-32B ($0.462$) beats Next-80B ($0.587$); GPT-oss-20B ($0.418$) edges 120B ($0.481$). The two monotonic families (GLM-4.7$\to$5; Kimi-K2-Thinking$\to$K2.5) couple scale with a version upgrade. With $n{=}14$--$26$ per axis, per-cell Wilson CIs span $\pm 0.2$---descriptive, not powered per-property. \emph{(F4) Reasoning/MoE flagships share a P2--P3 co-occurrence.} GPT-oss-120B ($0.481$), DeepSeek-V3.2 ($0.486$), Kimi-K2-Thinking ($0.434$), and Qwen3-Next-80B ($0.587$) share P2 weakness ($\hat T_{P_2}\!\in\![0.45,0.60]$) and severe P3 weakness ($\hat T_{P_3}\!\in\![0.14,0.27]$)---a safety regression tied to broader instruction-following degradation that any single-score benchmark erases.

\paragraph{Layer-4 subset validation.}
Re-evaluating on the 40-scenario subset chosen by the Layer-4 reference implementation (\S\ref{subsubsec:layer4_reference}), per-model RWF rises uniformly (the objective up-weights high-risk/high-complexity) but cross-model rank holds at Kendall-$\tau=0.890$, Spearman-$\rho=0.963$ vs.\ the full-100---a cheaper ranking-preserving route (Table~\ref{tab:layer4_validation}).

\subsection{Anti-Scaling Statistical Test}
\label{subsec:antiscaling_stats}

For each (smaller, larger) sibling pair we form the per-scenario RWF contribution $\rho_s(a)=\mathbb{1}[s\in V(a)]\,r(s)/\sum_{t\in Q} r(t)$ and paired difference $d_s = \rho_s(\text{larger}) - \rho_s(\text{smaller})$, then test $H_0\!:\,\mathbb{E}[d_s]\le 0$ with a paired bootstrap ($B{=}10{,}000$, seed~42).

\input{Chapter/_antiscaling_stats_inc.tex}

Two pairs reject at $p<0.05$ (Llama $p<0.001$, Qwen $p=0.004$); Mistral is marginal ($p=0.050$); GPT-oss does not reject ($p=0.086$). The pattern is unlikely pure sampling noise. This is a within-family single-suite test: it does not establish that scaling \emph{causes} the regression (concurrent changes in training data, alignment, or chat template are confounded with scale).

\subsection{Cross-Model Factory Generalisation}
\label{subsec:cross_model_treated}

We apply the identical Treated configuration (same firewall, same gate, no per-model tuning) to all 13 systems. The interventions were designed without observing any of these systems, so improvement is direct evidence of cross-model transfer (Table~\ref{tab:delta}, Figure~\ref{fig:before_after}).

\input{Chapter/_delta_table_inc.tex}

\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/before_after_rwf.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Factory cycle generalisation across 13 cross-family systems.} The interventions identified from Qwen3-8B's failure profile are applied uniformly with no per-model tuning. Red: Control RWF; green: Treated RWF. Sorted by Control RWF ascending.}
\label{fig:before_after}
\end{figure*}

\emph{Interventions transfer to 12 of 13 systems} ($\Delta\mathrm{RWF}\ge 0.13$). Largest gains occur on the weakest Control systems: GPT-oss-120B ($0.481 \to 0.000$, $\Delta=+0.481$), GPT-oss-20B ($0.418 \to 0.014$, $+0.404$), Mistral-Large-3 ($0.396 \to 0.101$, $+0.295$), Llama-3.1-70B ($0.407 \to 0.115$, $+0.292$), DeepSeek-V3.2 ($0.486 \to 0.205$, $+0.281$). Two systems reach zero detected violations under Treated (GPT-oss-120B, Llama-3.1-8B)---a ceiling effect of an L1 sandbox, not deployment-perfect behaviour. We read this as suggestive evidence that interventions from a single small open-weight model can reduce risk across architectures, providers, and tiers, but stop short of claiming model-agnosticism since one system is intervention-resistant and transfer is tested on a single shared sandbox.

\emph{Qwen3-32B is intervention-resistant}, improving marginally ($0.462 \to 0.443$, $\Delta=+0.019$) while every other system drops by $\geq 0.13$. Its failures concentrate on P3 ($\hat T_{P_3}=0.09$) and P4 ($0.11$), mostly \emph{direct user-instructed} disclosures and bias requests rather than indirect injections---failure modes the tool-output firewall cannot address. The profile surfaces what a scalar leaderboard would average away, identifying Qwen3-32B as needing a fundamentally different second cycle (policy fine-tuning or value alignment, not prompt-level guardrails).

\subsection{Annotation Reliability via LLM-Judge}
\label{subsec:annotation_reliability}

All numbers above rest on a heuristic label (per-scenario success-criterion check plus a refusal-language classifier on the final answer). We audit it via an LLM-judge study on a stratified 150-trajectory sample across the 13 systems and both configurations (30 per property, balanced). The judge is \textbf{Kimi-K2.5} via Bedrock Converse, returning JSON with \texttt{did\_violate}, \texttt{refusal\_type}, \texttt{is\_improper\_refusal}, and \texttt{confidence}. Kimi-K2.5 is itself one of the 13 evaluated agents---a deliberate scope limitation.

\input{Chapter/_llmjudge_validation_inc.tex}

On binary Violation, the judge agrees with the heuristic on $0.607$ of trajectories: Cohen's $\kappa=0.287$, weak ``fair'' agreement at the lower end of the Landis--Koch band. Agreement is substantial only on P4 ($\kappa=0.800$) and slight on P5 ($0.118$) and P1 ($0.143$), exactly the properties where the stratified violation pool is small (4 and 6), inflating chance agreement. The IR audit is more pointed: the judge marks 21/150 trajectories as improper refusals while the heuristic flags 0---recall~$0$ and undefined precision, direct evidence the heuristic cannot detect IR by surface-form match alone. The judge's \texttt{refusal\_type} labels place 30/38 ($\approx 79\%$) of non-\textsc{none} refusals on Treated agents, consistent with the safety-tax direction of \S\ref{subsec:cross_model_treated}. Two caveats: single judge without human gold, and shared family with two evaluated agents (Kimi-K2-Thinking, K2.5)---a separate-family judge plus a human spot-check on the 21 IR-flagged cases is planned.
