\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Current agent evaluation remains fragmented across benchmark islands, and the very target of evaluation---``trustworthiness''---is rarely defined in operational terms. We give a deployment-oriented definition through five measurable properties (Reliability, Robustness, Safety, Social-Ethical Alignment, Operational Integrity), and we introduce HAAF, which combines distribution-aware sampling with multi-layer probing and an iterative \emph{Trustworthy Optimization Factory} aligned to these properties. Our illustrative instantiation shows three results in concert: a 24-scenario Factory cycle on a single focal model narrows that model's vulnerability surface from 16.7\% to 4.2\% with no new improper refusals; a 100-scenario cross-model trustworthiness profile over 13 contemporary agentic systems from seven families surfaces property-level trade-offs no scalar leaderboard could capture, including a near-universal P3 (Safety) and P4 (Social-Ethical) weakness when violations originate from legitimate-looking user instructions rather than adversarial injections, and an \emph{anti-scaling} pattern in which larger / newer siblings within four different families fail to outperform their smaller / older counterparts; and the focal-model interventions, applied uniformly to those 13 systems on the 100-scenario suite, drive all 13 to better risk-weighted profiles (two to a perfect $\mathrm{RWF}=0.000$), with one intervention-resistant case (Qwen3-32B) that the profile pinpoints as a candidate for a fundamentally different second Factory cycle---direct evidence that property-targeted hardening identified by HAAF transfers across model families while preserving visibility into where it does not. Without representative, property-aware evaluation, benchmark progress will remain only weakly connected to deployment trustworthiness; we hope this work motivates a broader agenda around scenario modelling, risk-aware sampling, and iterative deployment-readiness assessment for agentic information-retrieval systems and beyond.
