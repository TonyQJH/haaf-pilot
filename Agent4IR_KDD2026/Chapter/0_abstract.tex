
Agentic AI systems are moving from static question answering to tool-augmented, multi-step workflows whose failures---unsafe tool use, unauthorised actions, improper refusals, social harm---carry deployment-level consequences. Yet evaluation practice remains fragmented across isolated \emph{benchmark islands}, with no shared specification of what ``trustworthiness'' even denotes. We address this in six steps. \textbf{First}, we \emph{define} agentic trustworthiness as a five-property profile---Reliability, Robustness, Safety, Social-Ethical Alignment, and Operational Integrity (P1--P5)---in which \emph{Improper Refusal} is a first-class taxonomy class mapped to P1, not a side metric. \textbf{Second}, we \emph{evaluate} 13 contemporary models across seven families (Llama, Mistral, Kimi, GLM, Qwen, GPT-oss, DeepSeek) on a 100-scenario validation suite, producing 2{,}600 Control/Treated trajectories. \textbf{Third}, we \emph{instantiate} the framework end-to-end on a single shared sandbox: red-team attribution of property-level failures, blue-team interventions targeted at those failures, and re-evaluation showing partial transfer to all 13 systems (12 substantially improve; one, Qwen3-32B, is intervention-resistant). \textbf{Fourth}, we release a Layer-4 distribution-aware sampling \emph{reference implementation}: a 40-scenario subset selected by the objective preserves the cross-model ranking (Kendall-$\tau$\,=\,0.890, Spearman-$\rho$\,=\,0.963). \textbf{Fifth}, we report Wilson 95\%~CIs on per-property success rates and paired-scenario bootstrap (B\,=\,10{,}000) on four intra-family anti-scaling pairs; three of four reject the larger-is-no-worse null at $p<0.05$ (per-property $n{=}14$--$26$ keeps per-cell CIs at roughly $\pm 0.2$, so these statistics are descriptive of cross-family trends rather than powered for fine-grained per-property claims). \textbf{Sixth}, we are explicit about scope: scenarios and sandbox tools are hand-authored, IR validation uses a single LLM-judge (Kimi-K2.5, which is itself one of the 13 evaluated agents and so shares blind spots with two Kimi-family systems), and scenario weights are not yet grounded in production traces; a deployment-grounded sampler is committed as v2.
